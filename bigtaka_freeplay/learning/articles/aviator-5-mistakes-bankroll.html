Mistake 1: Playing without a cash-out rule

Walk into any tea stall in Dhaka where Aviator is open on a phone, and you'll see the same scene — the plane is at 3×, the player says "ektu more, ektu more", and the plane disappears. Bet gone. This is the most common Aviator mistake in Bangladesh, and it's 100% preventable.

The fix is brutal in its simplicity: decide your cash-out multiplier before you bet. Not while the plane is climbing. Not after the round starts. Before. And use the built-in auto-cashout field so the decision is taken out of your hands.

  • Conservative target: 1.30× — hits ~76% of rounds
  • Balanced target: 1.50× — hits ~64% of rounds
  • Aggressive target: 2.00× — hits ~48% of rounds

Pick one and stick with it for at least 50 rounds before you change anything. If you're new to crash games, the full breakdown is in our Aviator beginner guide.

Mistake 2: Chasing losses with bigger bets

You lose 200 taka on a 1.20× crash. Next round, you bet 400 to "make it back." That round crashes too. Now you're 600 taka down and the round 3 bet is 800. This is the classic loss-chasing spiral and it's the fastest known way to turn a 2000 taka bankroll into zero.

Here's why chasing fails at the math level: Aviator rounds are independent. The probability that round 3 crashes early is exactly the same as round 1. You're not "owed" a win. You're just exposing more capital to the same risk.

The bankroll math doesn't care about your feelings. It cares about your bet size. Keep the bet size flat — your emotional swings can't compound into financial ruin.

The fix: Flat-betting. Same bet size every round, regardless of outcome. If your unit is 50 taka, it's 50 taka after a 10-round losing streak and 50 taka after a 5× win. Boring. Effective. Survives the variance.

Mistake 3: Doubling after every loss (the Martingale trap)

Bet 50. Lose. Bet 100. Lose. Bet 200. Lose. Bet 400. The theory is that one win recovers all losses plus a small profit. The theory is correct. The problem is the real world.

By round 7 you need to bet 3,200 taka just to be one win away from break-even. Hitting a 7-round losing streak at 1.50× target happens roughly once every 130 sessions — not "rare." It's a Tuesday. And when it happens, either your bankroll runs out or the table cap stops you. Either way: total wipeout.

The fix: never double your stake on a loss. The only safe way to vary your bet is to reduce it during a losing run, not grow it. Read our bankroll management rules for the full framework.

Mistake 4: Reading the history panel like tea leaves

The Aviator screen shows the last 50-100 multipliers. New players stare at it looking for "patterns." They see four 1.05× crashes in a row and think "a big one is coming." They see five 5×+ rounds and think "the streak will continue."

This is the gambler's fallacy, and Aviator's RNG is provably-fair — every round is generated by a fresh seed, independently of the previous 10,000 rounds. The history panel is decoration. It exists to make the game feel readable. It is not readable.

Shob theke important kotha: the past has zero predictive power on the next round. Treat the history panel like a clock. Glance at it. Don't strategize from it.

Mistake 5: Playing without a session stop-loss or stop-win

You sat down with 2,000 taka. You're up 1,800. You keep playing. Two hours later, you're down 1,200. The day's profit is gone — plus some.

This is the silent killer. Aviator rounds last 15-25 seconds, which means you can play 200 rounds an hour without noticing. Without a hard stop, variance always wins.

The fix is two numbers you decide before you log in:

  1. Stop-loss: 30-50% of your session bankroll. Down that much? Close the app. Tomorrow exists.
  2. Stop-win: 50-100% of your session bankroll. Up that much? Cash out the win, close the app. Banked profit is real. On-screen profit isn't.

Write them on paper. Tape them above your phone. The discipline is the strategy.

The 5-fix stack (your new pre-session checklist)

Before every Aviator session, run through this list. Takes 30 seconds. Saves thousands of taka.

  1. Set auto-cashout at 1.30×-1.50×. Lock it.
  2. Flat bet size. Same amount every round. No exceptions.
  3. No doubling. Losing streaks don't change your bet.
  4. Ignore the history panel. It's decoration.
  5. Stop-loss + stop-win written down. Honor them.
