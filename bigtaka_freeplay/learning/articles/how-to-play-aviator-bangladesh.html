How a round works (step by step)

Every Aviator round follows the same five-step rhythm:

  1. Place your bet. You'll see two betting panels — yes, you can bet twice on the same round. Type your bet amount or use the quick-bet buttons.
  2. Round starts. A plane takes off from the bottom-left. A multiplier ticker starts at 1.00× and climbs.
  3. Watch the multiplier. The longer the plane flies, the higher the multiplier — 1.50×, 2×, 5×, sometimes 100× or more.
  4. Cash out (or don't). Tap the big "Cash Out" button at any time. Your bet × the current multiplier hits your wallet immediately.
  5. The plane flies away. Eventually — could be at 1.01×, could be at 1000× — the plane disappears. Anyone still betting loses their stake.

That's the entire game. Every round is independent. The previous 10 results have zero impact on the next one (more on that in the mistakes section).

RTP & what it really means for you

Aviator's official RTP (Return to Player) is 97%. That's higher than almost every slot in the casino. It does not mean you'll get back 97 taka of every 100 you bet — that's a common misread.

What 97% RTP really means: across millions of bets, the average player gets back 97% of what they put in. In a single session of 50 rounds, you might be up 300% or down 100%. RTP is a long-game number, not a session-game number.

Why this matters: Aviator's high RTP makes it one of the least house-edge games in any Bangladesh casino. Compared to the average slot (96% RTP) or Lightning Roulette (97.30% on red/black, but only 88% on straight numbers), Aviator gives you better expected returns — provided you cash out smart. The auto-cashout trick (most beginners miss this)

Aviator has a built-in feature most new players never touch: auto cashout. You set a multiplier in advance — say, 1.50× — and the game automatically cashes you out the moment the plane hits it. You don't have to be looking at the screen.

Why it matters:

  • Removes greed. If you've decided 1.50× is enough, you won't be tempted to "let it ride to 2×" — the auto-cashout takes the decision out of your hands.
  • Removes panic. If your wifi lags or you tap the wrong button, the auto-cashout still fires.
  • Lets you go faster. You can run two bets per round with two different auto-cashout points — say 1.30× (safe) and 5× (moonshot). One usually hits.
The auto-cashout setting is the single biggest separator between players who lose money slowly and players who don't lose money. Use it from round one.

3 starter strategies for Bangladesh players

1. The "1.50× and stop" rule

Set auto-cashout to 1.50×. Bet the same amount every round. Your win rate will be roughly 60% — the plane crashes above 1.50× about 6 out of 10 rounds. Multiply your win amount × 0.50, subtract your loss amount × 1.00, and you'll usually end the session slightly up or break-even. This is the boring, sustainable strategy.

2. The split-bet strategy

Aviator lets you place two bets per round. Set bet 1 to auto-cashout at 1.30× (the safe one — hits ~78% of the time). Set bet 2 to auto-cashout at 5× (the moonshot — hits ~17% of the time). Most rounds, bet 1 covers bet 2's loss. Once a session, bet 2 lands and turns the day green.

3. The bankroll-protect strategy

Decide your session limit before you start. Example: 1000 taka. Divide by 50 rounds = 20 taka per bet. Auto-cashout at 1.40×. If you hit -300 taka, stop. If you hit +400 taka, stop. Walking away with a profit is the skill that separates winners from grinders. Full bankroll guide →

3 mistakes new Bangladesh players make in their first week

1. "The plane crashed early 5 times — it's due for a big multiplier"

It's not. Aviator uses a provably-fair RNG. Every round is independent. The probability of the next round crashing at 1.20× is exactly the same whether the last 10 rounds all crashed at 1.05× or all crashed at 50×. This is called the gambler's fallacy, and it's the #1 way Bangladesh players blow their bankrolls.

2. "I'll just let it ride to 10×, then cash out"

Sounds great. Hits about 9% of the time. Meaning: 91% of those rounds, you lose your full bet. Without auto-cashout, you'll also catch yourself hesitating — "just one more second" — and miss the cash out by 0.1 seconds. Always set the cashout BEFORE the plane takes off.

3. "I lost 5 in a row, time to double my bet"

This is the Martingale system. It works in theory. In practice, you hit your table limit (or your bankroll limit) after 6-7 consecutive losses — which absolutely happens. We wrote a full breakdown of why Martingale fails on Aviator →
